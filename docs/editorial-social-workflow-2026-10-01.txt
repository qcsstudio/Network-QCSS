QCS CONTENT AND SOCIAL WORKFLOW
Research and implementation review: 1 October 2026

DECISION

One reviewed source article, one topic-specific visual concept, and separate
platform adaptations. Reuse the evidence, not a generic paragraph or picture.
Blogs/resources still require admin approval. Security advisories retain their
automatic technical-review path. Social distribution cannot publish a draft,
an obsolete revision, an unreviewed caption or a mismatched image.

WHAT THE AUDIT FOUND AND CHANGED

1. Blog artwork could fall back to a reusable procedural renderer even though
   advisories had recently moved to contextual concepts. Both now require an
   evidence-mapped concept, independent visual review and an approved master.
   Provider failures and exhausted budgets do not become generic artwork.
2. The image writer was still told to force every article into three frames.
   Establish/explain/resolve now describes reasoning, not a prescribed layout.
3. LinkedIn writer failures could receive a fabricated 88/100 fallback score.
   This fallback is removed. Unfinished copy stays queued for actual review.
4. LinkedIn research input silently excluded later sections and clipped their
   bodies. It now includes the complete body and its late qualifications.
5. Queued social images could resolve to the newest image instead of the
   reviewed revision. Delivery now uses an asset-specific endpoint, generation
   timestamp and current-publication check. No paid work occurs on that endpoint.
6. Advisory share metadata used the generic homepage image. It now points to
   the advisory image. QCS publication dates and vendor disclosure dates are
   distinguished, including in article metadata.
7. Facebook/Instagram previously had no delivery path. They now have separate
   captions, account verification, durable jobs, previews and API receipts.

BLOG / RESOURCE CREATION

Discovery: rank relevant audience questions from vendor documentation, release
notes, standards bodies, security authorities, engineering publications, news
and search-demand signals. Trends suggest questions; they are not evidence.

Research: read full primary material and relevant version-specific documents.
Use related discussions to identify practical questions, disagreements and
edge cases. Do not claim an exhaustive search of the internet. Preserve the
source URL, retrieval date, key finding and evidence used for each claim.
Unavailable pages and conflicting claims remain explicit gaps, not invented facts.

Draft structure:
- A literal title and a direct, self-contained answer.
- Who this affects, prerequisites and a plain-English explanation of terms.
- The mechanism or problem, with citations beside the claims they support.
- A worked example with assumptions, not an invented customer success story.
- Options and trade-offs, followed by safe implementation guidance.
- Expected observations, validation, common failures and rollback boundaries.
- A useful takeaway, genuinely relevant FAQs and authoritative references.

Review: check accuracy, evidence coverage, practical value, first-use definitions,
unsafe commands, contradictions, topic drift, repetition and unsupported claims.
Keep independent critique, deterministic checks and the admin approval gate.
AI scores are editorial signals, not a guarantee of factual perfection.

Search: canonical URLs, meaningful headings, crawlable text, descriptive image
alternatives, useful internal links and accurate Article/BlogPosting data.
Do not claim that special AIO markup, fixed word counts or guaranteed rankings
exist. The current 1,000-word QCS guide gate is an editorial format choice,
not a Google requirement. Thin announcements should not be padded into guides.

SECURITY ADVISORIES

Keep vendor disclosure, hardening release, advance notice and confirmed
vulnerability distinct. Preserve the exact affected-product/configuration and
release-to-fix mapping. Do not turn an absent CVSS into zero or an unspecified
workaround into a claim that no workaround exists.

Explain: what happened; who is affected; why it matters; the technical mechanism;
what is known about exploitation; what the team should do; how to verify the
change; and what remains unknown. Separate a temporary mitigation from a fix.

CVSS, vendor severity, KEV inclusion, EPSS probability and local exposure are
different inputs. None is interchangeable with confirmed exploitation.
Unknown ransomware involvement is not a statement that exploitation is unknown.
Do not imply that applying a patch proves the environment was never compromised.

Advisories may publish automatically only after existing source and technical
gates pass. Failed checks are held visibly. Website availability must not depend
on a social API succeeding. Corrections create a new reviewed revision.

IMAGE CONCEPT AND CONSISTENCY

Read the complete article and identify its actual subject, relationship,
mechanism, uncertainty and operator decision. Propose distinct compositions,
choose one and explain why it teaches this specific story.

Examples of reasoning, NOT presets:
- RPKI: origin authorization compared with an observed advertisement; do not
  imply that origin validation proves the entire AS path or prevents all hijacks.
- Firewall authentication flaw: the specific management/authentication boundary
  in the source; do not invent a successful breach or vendor interface.
- Cloud routing article: the relevant route/permission boundary and trade-off,
  not a generic cloud icon surrounded by unrelated servers.
- DNS cold start: the actual resolver/cache sequence and timing assumption,
  not a security shield with DNS substituted into the headline.

Require two to five exact evidence excerpts mapped to visible relationships.
Reject a concept that only changes the title, CVE, vendor, colour or logo.
Compare recent scene descriptions and signatures before rendering. Compare a
256-bit perceptual hash after rendering, as an additional reuse warning.
This heuristic is not a semantic proof; older assets without hashes are covered
by their concept records and visual critique until new hashes accumulate.

Generate a text-free master; add the authentic QCS logo in code. Keep exact CVEs,
versions and factual wording in accessible captions/body text rather than asking
a raster model to spell them. New artwork is not presented as an incident photo
or tested network measurement. Instagram receives the supported AI disclosure.

Web: 1440x810. LinkedIn/Facebook: 1200x627. Instagram: the complete 1440x810 JPEG
master, which fits the documented ratio and width limits, instead of cutting
the sides into a portrait. A portrait/carousel with custom annotation remains
a later, separately reviewed composition, not a stretched version of this image.
No extra image-generation charge is needed for these channel derivatives.

There is one paid render per attempt. Failed paid renders require an explicit
retry. Existing configurable daily/monthly limits remain in force. They count
stored generation records rather than serving as a transactional billing ledger;
concurrent attempts or manual retries can exceed an intended monetary ceiling.
Keep provider-level spend limits too. This work does not silently raise budgets,
generate the archive again, or call an image provider during testing.

SOCIAL ADAPTATIONS

LinkedIn: one operational point of view, source-supported specifics, implications,
concrete actions and verification, with short paragraphs and a complete original
QCS URL plus focused hashtags. Keep the existing lossless little-text escaping
and 2,900-character safety limit. Native feed posts do not provide normal rich
bold/italic formatting; avoid inaccessible Unicode pseudo-bold and raw Markdown.

Facebook: a plain-language consequence for the team or business, a compact
explanation and practical checks. Include the QCS link and three focused tags.

Instagram: one understandable learning takeaway, useful actions and a clear
qualification. Include 3-5 relevant tags and the original QCS URL, but do not
promise that a caption link is clickable or claim the bio was updated. Readers
are also directed to search the article title on qcsstudio.com.

Meta captions have their own direct OpenAI writer and independent reviewer.
Exact excerpt mappings plus the reviewer check the claims. Length validation
rejects excessive text instead of cutting off the footer. A failed review does
not receive an invented passing score or fall back to generic copy.

Setup:
1. Create/configure a Meta developer app for the QCS-owned accounts. Keep its
   business details and privacy/deletion information accurate. Request only the
   relevant permissions and complete any required App Review/access process.
2. Authorize a Facebook Page token for an operator with CREATE_CONTENT or MANAGE.
   Facebook photo posting requires pages_manage_posts, pages_read_engagement and
   pages_show_list. Instagram Facebook Login requires instagram_basic and
   instagram_content_publish as well as pages_read_engagement. Business Manager
   role arrangements can add permission requirements; verify these in Meta.
3. Obtain the Page ID and linked professional Instagram ID through Meta. Store
   the token and app secret securely. Respect token expiration and revocation.
   This release uses server-managed credentials, not a new OAuth setup wizard.
4. Set the activation timestamp to avoid a historical-posting flood. Keep
   automatic delivery disabled. In Admin > Distribution, Verify accounts checks
   that the token's Page and its linked Instagram identity match the settings.
5. Prepare next builds a preview for an eligible published article. Inspect the
   entire caption and image. Fix any hold before proceeding.
6. Set META_PUBLISHING_ENABLED=1 only after verification. Process the queue for
   a controlled first delivery. Verify the API receipt and actual live post.
7. Enable GitHub repository variable QCS_META_PUBLISHING_ENABLED=true to run the
   added Meta job in the existing editorial workflow. It invokes the protected
   /api/cron/social-publisher?channel=meta endpoint with the existing OIDC identity.
   No extra Vercel cron entry, paid queue product or AI gateway was added.

The scheduler advances one Meta job per invocation. Instagram upload and publish
are separate invocations; the container ID is saved and reused. The API quota
is checked at runtime instead of trusting inconsistent numeric examples in docs.
Five bounded processing checks precede a hold. API rejection, expiry and token
problems remain visible. A final-call timeout or interrupted dispatch requires
manual reconciliation in Business Suite, not a blind repost. The admin cannot
reset an uncertain delivery into the publishing queue.

The current UI supports preview, prepare, process, cancel waiting delivery and
retry pre-dispatch preparation. It does not delete live Meta posts or automatically
backfill historical posts. It does not yet provide automated reconciliation of an
uncertain receipt; inspect Business Suite and reconcile the record explicitly.

Web publication precedes social delivery. Jobs are unique by channel/content/
revision, and the first automatic Meta post per content item is discovered after
activation. Article revisions do not trigger unsolicited duplicate reposts.
Published source status, revision and matching image lineage are checked again
before dispatch. Drafts and missing/deleted sources are not delivered.

COST AND HONEST LIMITS

No new paid hosting service is required by these changes. Existing database,
GitHub Actions, Vercel, OpenAI and BFL usage limits still apply. This is not a
promise of unlimited free operation. Each prepared Meta caption uses a writer
and a critic call; successful copy is cached in the job. No new image is generated
per platform. Scheduled jobs may be delayed; five-minute configuration is not a
five-minute delivery SLA. No real Meta publishing or paid AI generation was run
as part of local verification. Credential and account checks are complete;
deployment and a controlled live smoke test are still required before calling
the integration operational.
